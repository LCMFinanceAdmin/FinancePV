Claiming money back

Page 1 of 3

You paid for something and want the church to pay you back — petrol, refreshments, a repair. Anyone may do this: pastors, EXCO members, volunteers. You do not have to be on the payroll.

Sending the claim

Left-hand menu → Submit PV
  1. Fill in who is being paid.If the money comes back to you, that is your own name, bank and account number. Check the account number twice — a wrong digit is the commonest reason a payment fails.
  2. Choose the ministry, and the project if there is one.This decides whose budget it comes from. If you are unsure, ask your EXCO member before sending rather than guessing.
  3. Write the purpose.One line. “Petrol, Kajang visitation, 12–14 Aug” is better than “Claim”.
  4. If it is a personal allowance, say which.A picker sits under Purpose — dental, books, moving. It then tells you what is left of that allowance. Leave it alone for an ordinary payment from a ministry budget.
  5. List what you spent, line by line.A date, a description and an amount for each. For travel use the mileage rows: from, to, and kilometres. It works out RM0.70 per km for you.
  6. Attach the receipts.Photographs from your phone are fine. A claim with no receipts comes back to you.
  7. Tick the confirmation, type your name, and submit.You get a voucher number — PV-2026-xxx. Write it down, or find it again under My Submissions.

Following it afterwards

Left-hand menu → My Space → My Submissions
Stuck at one stage for a while? Open the voucher — it names the person it is waiting on. Ask them directly. Nothing moves until they act.
Rejected is not cancelled. It has come back to you with a reason. Correct what was asked and submit it again; you do not start from nothing.

Leave, and your payslip

Page 2 of 3

Both of these are for people employed by LCM. If you are a volunteer these will not appear in your menu, and that is correct rather than broken.

Applying for leave

Left-hand menu → My Space → My Leave
  1. Look at the Balance tab first.Every kind of leave, with what you have used and what is left. Annual and sick leave both rise with your length of service, so the figures are yours, not everybody’s.
  2. Press Apply for Leave and pick the type.A type marked with a star needs a supporting document — a medical certificate, for instance.
  3. Enter the first and last day.The app counts the working days and ignores weekends. Check the number it shows before going on.
  4. Give a reason, and attach the document if one is needed.
  5. Read who it is going to.The form names them before you send. If it says no approver could be worked out, stop and ask Finance to check your record.
  6. Sign and submit.Your balance at that moment is stored on the application, so your approvers see the same figures you saw.
  7. Watch it under the Pending tab.It lists who has signed and who has not.
Seven days’ notice for annual leave. Less than that shows a warning but does not stop you. Your approvers can see the dates and judge for themselves.

Getting your payslip

Left-hand menu → My Space → My Salary
  1. Find the month under Payslips.One row per month, newest first, with the net and gross beside it.
  2. Press Download.The PDF is built there and then. Past months stay available, so a lost payslip is never a problem.
  3. While you are there, check Details on file.Your bank account number is on that page. It is the account your salary goes into.
A month missing? It has not been finalised yet — figures still being prepared are held back on purpose. The page says no payroll record is linked? Your sign-in address has not been matched to your salary record; ask Finance, it is one edit for them.

Verifying a claim — EXCO

  1. Read the card without opening anything.The four things you need are on its face: the ministry, the amount, who is being paid, and what for. The budget line and what is left of it are there too.
  2. Check the line can carry it.The card shows what has been spent and what is committed but unpaid. Use the lower figure.
  3. Press Verify, or Reject with a reason.The reason is what the claimant sees, so write it for them. Verifying sends it on to Finance.
You will never be asked to verify your own claim. One raised by the head of a ministry skips their own review and goes straight to Finance.

Signing a voucher — Bishop, Secretary, Treasurer

  1. Read the card.Ministry, amount, payee, purpose and the effect on the budget, without opening the voucher. On a phone these stack like banking approvals.
  2. Open it if you want the detail.Every line, the receipts, and everyone who has already signed. The signing area is below the voucher, so you scroll through what you are signing.
  3. Approve with the tick, or reject.You will be asked for your PIN. That PIN puts your name on the record — do not give it to anyone.
  4. For a batch, use All.Or open and sign the individual vouchers inside it one at a time.
Why two signatures sometimes. Up to RM30,000 the Treasurer signs alone. Above it, any two of the Bishop, Secretary and Treasurer. An asset over RM100,000 goes to EXCO. Set by the amount — nobody chooses it.

Approving leave

  1. Open the application.It shows the days, the dates, the reason, any document, and the applicant’s annual and medical balance as it stood when they applied. You need not look anything up.
  2. Sign to approve, or reject with remarks.
Everyone named must sign, and order does not matter. If three people are listed, all three sign; whoever opens it first may sign first. Nobody is a rubber stamp, and nobody signs their own leave — if you would otherwise approve your own, you are removed and the others settle it.
Nothing in your queue that you expected? The menu is built from your role, so a missing queue is a duty that is not yours. If you believe that is wrong, ask Finance to check your record rather than assuming a fault.
