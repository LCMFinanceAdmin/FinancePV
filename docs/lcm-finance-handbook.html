One rule worth knowing first

Anyone may claim money back — pastors, EXCO members, volunteers. Being owed money is not an employment matter. Leave and staff loans are different: they are employment entitlements, so they appear only for people actually employed by LCM. A volunteer EXCO member has an @lcm.org.my address but will not see My Leave.

Claiming money back

You paid for something out of your own pocket — petrol, refreshments, a repair — and you want the church to pay you back. Use Submit PV, which sits at the top of the menu for everyone.

  1. Open Submit PV.It is pinned to the top of the left-hand menu, under the Dashboard.
  2. Fill in who is being paid.If the money is coming back to you, that is your own name, your own bank and your own account number. The form is in English and Chinese, side by side.
  3. Choose the ministry and project.This decides whose budget the money comes out of, so it matters. If you are not sure, ask your EXCO member before submitting rather than guessing.
  4. List what you spent, line by line.One line per item, each with a date, a description and an amount. For travel there is a separate mileage section with "from" and "to".
  5. Attach your receipts.A claim without receipts will come back to you. Photographs from your phone are fine.
  6. Sign and submit.Tick the confirmation and type your name. You will get a voucher number — write it down, or find it later under My Space → My Submissions.

What happens next

Your claim now travels through a fixed chain. You can watch it at any time under My Space → My Submissions. These are the exact words you will see on the screen:

  1. Pending EXCO Review Waiting for the EXCO member responsible for that ministry. This stage is skipped if the ministry has no head recorded, or if you are that head.
  2. Pending Finance Review With the Finance Executive, who checks the receipts, the budget line and the bank details.
  3. Ministry Verified Checked and ready for signature.
  4. Pending Signatory With the Treasurer — and with a second officer if the amount is above RM30,000. See below.
  5. Approved Signed. Now waiting for Finance to actually make the payment.
  6. Paid The money has gone out, and the payment reference is on the voucher.

How many signatures your claim needs

This is set by the church's limits of authority, and the app applies it automatically from the amount. You do not choose it.

Up to RM30,000 One signature — the Treasurer.
Above RM30,000 Two signatures — any two of the Bishop, Secretary and Treasurer.
Assets over RM100,000 Two signatures, and it goes to EXCO.

Asking the church to pay someone else

If you have not spent the money yet and you want the church to pay a supplier from your ministry's budget, that is a different form: Payments → Payment Requests. It goes to your own EXCO first, then to the General Manager, and Finance raises the voucher for you at the end.

  1. Awaiting EXCO verificationYour ministry's own committee agrees the spending.
  2. With the General ManagerThe GM approves and instructs Finance.
  3. Approved — Finance raising PVFinance prepares the voucher.
  4. PV raisedFrom here it follows the signature chain above. Because your EXCO has already verified it, it is not sent back to them a second time.
Which form do I use?

Money already spent, and you want it back — Submit PV.
Money not yet spent, and the church should pay directly — Payment Request.

Verifying your ministry's claims

For EXCO members and anyone appointed to verify on their behalf. Your committee's claims wait for you at Approvals → EXCO Queue, and nothing moves until you act.

  1. Open Approvals → EXCO Queue.Everything shown is waiting on you. The number on the menu item is the count.
  2. Read the card before you open anything.Each card already shows the ministry, the amount, who is being paid, what it is for, and how much of the budget line is left. That is deliberate — the four things you need are on the card.
  3. Check the budget line.The card shows what has been spent and what is still in flight. If a claim would take the line past its limit, that is your decision to make, not the app's.
  4. Press Verify or Reject.Verifying sends it onward. Rejecting sends it back to the person who raised it — always give a reason, because the reason is what they see.
You cannot verify your own claim

If you raise a claim for a ministry you head, it skips your own review and goes straight to Finance. Nobody verifies themselves.

Signing vouchers

For the Bishop, Secretary and Treasurer. Vouchers waiting for your signature are at Approvals → Signatory Queue.

  1. Open Approvals → Signatory Queue.On a phone this is a stack of approval cards, one per voucher, in the style of a banking app.
  2. Read the card.Ministry, amount, who is being paid, what it is for, and the effect on the budget — all on the face of the card, without opening the voucher.
  3. Open the voucher if you want the detail.The full voucher shows every line, the receipts, and everyone who has already signed. The signing area sits below the voucher, so you scroll through what you are signing before you sign it.
  4. Approve with the tick, or reject.You will be asked for your PIN. That PIN is what puts your name on the record, so do not give it to anyone.
  5. For a batch, you can approve all at once.Bulk vouchers show an All button. You can still open and sign the individual vouchers inside a batch one by one if you prefer.

Everything you have ever acted on stays under Payments → My Approvals.

Your salary and payslips

Everything about your own pay is at My Space → My Salary. You do not need to ask anybody, and you do not need to wait for an email.

  1. Open My Space → My Salary.It shows only your own record. Nobody else's pay is visible to you, and yours is not visible to anyone except Finance and the General Manager.
  2. Read your current monthly pay.Basic salary and each allowance separately, with the date it took effect.
  3. Check the salary history.Every change ever made, with the date, the amount and the reason — so an increment can be checked rather than taken on trust.
  4. Download any payslip.One line per month. Press Download and the PDF is built there and then. Past months stay available, so a lost payslip is never a problem again.
  5. Check your bank account number under "Details on file".It is the account your salary is paid into, and a wrong digit is the commonest reason a payment fails.
Why the page is read-only

You can see everything and change nothing. A change to your own pay details — your bank account above all — always passes through a Finance Executive. Tell them what needs correcting.

If the page says no payroll record is linked

Most people are now connected — 61 of the 81 on payroll. If you are one of the remaining twenty, it is because the church has no @lcm.org.my address on file for you, so there is nothing to recognise you by. Give a Finance Executive your LCM address and the page will work at your next sign-in.

A payslip only appears once Finance has finalised that month's payroll. A run still being prepared is deliberately hidden, because the figures in it are still moving.

Claiming against one

  1. Raise the voucher as usual, at Submit PV.
  2. Choose the entitlement it draws on.There is a picker below Purpose. Leave it alone for an ordinary payment from a ministry budget — most vouchers are that, not a personal claim.
  3. Read what it tells you.It shows what is left of that allowance this year, and warns if your claim is above it. It does not stop you: the Terms have exceptions and your approvers see the same figures.
How "used" is counted

From your own vouchers, once they are approved or paid. A claim still working its way through the chain has not come off your balance yet, so the figure you see is what has actually been settled.

Allowances given per occasion rather than per year — moving, maternity — show no running total. The church has no way to tell which relocation or which birth a voucher belongs to, so what you see is the ceiling on a single claim.

Applying for leave

Go to My Space → My Leave. The page opens on your balance, and the button to apply is on the same screen.

What you are entitled to each year

Annual and sick leave both rise with your length of service. The same chart applies to pastors, parish workers and office staff alike.

Completed service Annual leave Sick leave
Under 2 years14 days14 days
2 to under 5 years14 days18 days
5 years14 days22 days
6 to 10 years21 days22 days
11 years and over25 days22 days

Annual leave follows the church's Terms and Conditions (A9.1, B8.1, C7.1); the two charts step at different years because sick leave follows the Employment Act instead, which the Terms are silent on.

The rest

  • 60Hospitalisation
  • 3Emergency
  • 98Maternity
  • 7Paternity
  • —Replacement (earned)

Compassionate, study and unpaid leave are also available and have no fixed yearly allowance. Replacement leave is only what you have earned by working on a rest day.

Hospitalisation is 60 days including your sick leave

Not 60 on top of it. If you have already taken 18 days of ordinary sick leave, 42 hospitalisation days remain, and the balance page shows it that way. Both count against the same 60.

Two rules that apply to annual leave only

Nothing in your first six months. Annual leave starts once you have completed six months of service.

Your first year is pro-rated. Join in July and you get half a year's worth. Neither rule touches sick leave, which you have in full from your first day.

  1. Open My Space → My Leave.The Balance tab shows each type of leave, how many days you have used and how many are left this year.
  2. Press Apply and choose the type of leave.A type marked with an asterisk needs a supporting document — a medical certificate, for example.
  3. Enter the first and last day.The app counts working days for you and ignores weekends. Check the number it shows before you continue.
  4. Give a reason, and attach the document if one is needed.
  5. Look at who it is going to.Before you send, the form names the people who will have to sign. If it says no approver could be worked out, stop and ask Finance to check your record.
  6. Sign and submit.Your balance at that moment is stored on the application, so the approvers see the same figures you saw.
Seven days' notice for annual leave

Applying for annual leave with less than seven days' notice shows a warning, but it does not stop you submitting. The rule has exceptions, and your approvers can see the dates and judge for themselves.

Track it under the Pending tab, which lists who has signed and who has not. Past applications stay under History.

Approving leave

Applications waiting on you are at Approvals → Leave Queue. Who has to sign depends on who is applying — a pastor's leave is not routed like a staff member's.

If the applicant is a Pastor
  1. Head pastor of their congregation
  2. Council Chairman or RepresentativeNot an LCM employee — signs through a one-time link sent to them by email
  3. Their district Dean
If the applicant is a Dean
  1. The Bishop

A Dean is also a pastor, but their own leave goes straight to the Bishop.

If the applicant is Staff
  1. The Bishop
  2. The General Manager, if they report to both

Set per person, so it matches how each member of staff actually reports.

Where the routing comes from

Who signs a pastor’s leave is worked out from the church they serve and the district that church sits in. Both are now on record — 49 churches across six districts — which is what lets the chain resolve without anybody typing it in. If your leave goes to the wrong people, the cause is almost always the church recorded against your name; ask Finance or the Administrator to correct it rather than working around it.

How the request reaches you

If you have a login — Dean, head pastor, GM, Bishop — you get an email with a button that opens that exact application, already at the top of your queue. You sign in as normal, because your approval is recorded against your identity.

If you are the Council Chairman, your email carries the pastor's name, the dates and the length, and one button that opens a page showing that single application. No account, no password. The link is personal to you and lasts 30 days.

Two things that surprise people

Everyone named must sign. Nobody on the list is a rubber stamp, and the order does not matter — whoever opens it first can sign first.

Nobody ever signs their own leave. If the applicant would otherwise be one of their own approvers — a head pastor applying for their own leave, for instance — they are removed from the list, and the remaining signatories settle it.

What you see when approving

Each application in your queue shows the number of days, the dates, the reason, any document attached, and the applicant's annual and medical balance as it stood when they applied. You do not need to look their balance up separately.

Seeing everyone's balances

A full list of every person's balance, and who has yet to sign, is at Administration → Leave Overview — available to the General Manager, the Administrator and the Finance Executive. Deans and head pastors see balances on the applications they are asked to sign.

The annual budget

For EXCO members and the Treasurer. Everything is at Budget → Ministry Budget. An EXCO member sees their own ministries; the Treasurer, GM, Bishop and Finance see them all.

Reading the budget

The budget is set out the way a set of accounts is: income above expenditure, sub-projects indented under the project they belong to, a subtotal under each section on a rule, and the ministry’s total under a double rule. Five columns of figures, all in ringgit:

ColumnWhat it counts
BudgetWhat the line was given for the year.
SpentVouchers approved and paid.
CommittedVouchers raised but not yet paid. The number in brackets is how many.
BalanceBudget less Spent. Ignores anything committed.
AvailableBudget less Spent less Committed.
Which balance to use

There are two on purpose. Use Available when deciding whether there is room for something new — the committed vouchers are still coming, and Balance does not know about them. Figures shown in red are over budget and in amber are close to it; a healthy line is left in ordinary ink so the ones needing attention are the ones that catch your eye.

Proposing your ministry's budget — EXCO

  1. Choose your ministry and the year.Lines you add for a future year belong to that year's proposal until the Treasurer approves them.
  2. Add each project as a line.Name, whether it is income or expense, the estimated amount, and any notes. You can attach the supporting paper to the line.
  3. Add sub-projects underneath a project if you need to.A line can sit under a parent line, so a large project can be broken down.
  4. Submit the proposal.It goes to the Treasurer as a whole — you do not submit lines one at a time.
  5. Wait. The budget is locked while it is with the Treasurer.This is intentional: it stops the figures moving underneath the person deciding on them.

Approving a budget — Treasurer

  1. Open Budget → Ministry Budget.Submitted proposals from every ministry are listed together, oldest first.
  2. Read the ministry's lines and the papers attached to them.
  3. Approve or reject, with a note.Approving makes the lines live, and claims can then be charged against them. Rejecting unlocks the budget so the ministry can revise it. Your note is what they see.

The Finance Executive can also decide a proposal. That is deliberate — it means a budget is never stuck if the Treasurer is away.

Changing a budget that is already approved

Once a budget is approved, an EXCO member cannot simply edit it. You ask, and someone with authority decides. The three kinds of change — add a line, edit a line, delete a line — all work the same way.

  1. You make the change and save You fill in the form exactly as if you were editing. Instead of saving, the app records it as a request and tells you so.
  2. The EXCO board is told Everyone holding an EXCO portfolio is emailed, along with the Treasurer, General Manager, Bishop, Secretary and Finance Executive. A change to an approved budget is the board's business, not a private exchange between your ministry and whoever opens the page next.
  3. Pending The request sits at the top of the budget page, visible to your ministry and to those who can decide it. Nothing has changed in the budget yet.
  4. Approved or rejected Decided by the Finance Executive, Treasurer, General Manager, Bishop or Secretary. An approved change is applied to the budget immediately. A rejected one keeps its reason on the record.

Questions people ask

My claim has said "Pending EXCO Review" for two weeks. What do I do?

It is sitting with the EXCO member for that ministry, and only they can move it. Open the voucher from My Space → My Submissions — it names the person it is waiting on. Ask them directly. If nobody is named, ask Finance to check that the ministry has a head recorded.

What is the difference between Submit PV and a Payment Request?

Submit PV is for money already spent, or a payment you know the full details of. It raises the voucher straight away.

Payment Request is for money not yet spent — you are asking your ministry and then the GM to agree to it, and Finance raises the voucher afterwards.

Why does my claim need two signatures when my colleague's needed one?

The amount. Up to RM30,000 the Treasurer alone signs. Above RM30,000 it needs any two of the Bishop, Secretary and Treasurer. An asset purchase above RM100,000 goes to EXCO. The app works this out from the amount — nobody chooses it.

My claim was rejected. Can I fix it and send it again?

Yes. The rejection carries the reason the approver typed. Correct what they asked for and submit again. Rejection is not a cancellation — it is being sent back to you.

How do I get my payslip?

Download it yourself from My Space → My Salary. Every finalised month is listed, and pressing Download builds the PDF on the spot. Finance can also still send it to you, but you no longer have to ask.

My Salary says no payroll record is linked to my login.

Your pay details exist — your sign-in address just hasn't been matched to them. Ask a Finance Executive to link your People Directory record to your payroll record. Until they do, the page is not permitted to show you anything, which is why it shows nothing rather than an error.

Last month's payslip isn't in the list.

That run has not been finalised yet. Figures in a run still being prepared can change, so they are held back rather than shown as though they were settled. It appears as soon as Finance closes the month.

Why does my leave need three signatures?

Because you are a pastor. Note 6(a) of the church's leave form requires the Council Chairman or Representative and the district Dean, and LCM practice adds the head pastor of your congregation. All three must sign, in any order.

Our Council Chairman has no login. How can they sign?

They do not need one. The Council Chairman holds a church council office, not an LCM post, so the app emails them a one-time link that lets them sign that single application and nothing else.

I applied for annual leave with four days' notice. Will it be rejected automatically?

No. You will see a warning about the seven-day rule, but the application still goes through. Your approvers can see the dates and decide. Nothing is blocked automatically.

I am a Dean. Who approves my leave?

The Bishop, and only the Bishop. You are a pastor too, but a Dean's own leave goes straight to the Bishop rather than round the congregation.

Maternity or paternity leave has disappeared from my list.

Each is now offered only to the people the Terms give it to — maternity to a co-worker who has given birth, paternity to one whose wife has. Which you see follows the gender on your directory record. If yours is blank you will see both, and if it is wrong, ask Finance or the Administrator to correct it on your profile.

My annual leave went up this year and nobody told me.

It rises with service: 14 days up to five years, 21 from six to ten, 25 from eleven. The app used to give everybody 14 regardless, so if you have been here more than five years you were being under-credited and are now getting what the Terms and Conditions always said. Sick leave works the same way on a different chart — 14, then 18 at two years, then 22 at five.

How much of my dental or book allowance is left?

My Space → My Salary lists every allowance with what you have used and what remains. The same figures appear on the Submit PV form once you pick which allowance a claim draws on.

I claimed something before and it is not counted.

Claims raised before the allowances were set up carry no tag, so they do not count against a balance. If that matters for your figures, tell Finance which vouchers were personal claims and they can mark them.

How do I see how many days I have left?

My Space → My Leave opens on the Balance tab, which shows every type of leave with days used and days remaining for the current year.

Can I see everyone's leave balance?

Only the General Manager, the Administrator and the Finance Executive have the full list, at Administration → Leave Overview. If you approve leave, you see the applicant's annual and medical balance on the application itself, as it stood when they applied.

Why can't I edit the budget directly? I am the EXCO member for that ministry.

Because it is already approved. An approved budget is a decision of the church, so changing it is a request, not an edit. Fill in the change exactly as if editing — the app records it as a request, tells you it has done so, and emails the EXCO board that you have asked. The Treasurer, General Manager, Bishop, Secretary or Finance Executive then decides it.

Our budget says it is locked and I cannot change anything.

It has been submitted and is with the Treasurer. It unlocks when they approve or reject it. This is deliberate — the figures should not move while somebody is deciding on them.

The budget shows two different balances. Which one do I use?

Balance counts only what has been approved and paid. Available also subtracts vouchers raised but not yet paid. Use Available — the lower one — when deciding whether there is room for new spending, because those vouchers are still coming.

I am an EXCO member but not employed by LCM. Why is there no My Leave for me?

Leave and staff loans are employment entitlements, so they only appear for people employed by the church. Claiming money back is not — you can still raise a voucher for anything you have paid for.

Can I approve my own claim if I am the EXCO member for that ministry?

No, and you will not be asked to. A claim raised by the head of a ministry skips their own review and goes straight to Finance.

My name shows the wrong title — Rev. when I am a Pastor, or the other way round.

The title is not typed anywhere; it comes from whether your record says you are ordained a Reverend or a Pastor. That was read from the church’s own contact list, which writes “Rev.” or “Ps.” in front of each name. If yours is wrong, the record is wrong — ask Finance or the Administrator to change the ordination on your directory entry and every screen follows.

My church, or my district, is wrong.

Ask Finance or the Administrator to correct it in the Church Directory. It is worth doing promptly: your church decides which head pastor and which Dean your leave goes to, so a wrong church quietly sends your applications to the wrong people.

Which of my email addresses does the church use?

Two are held. Your @lcm.org.my address is the one the app writes to — approval requests, payslips, notifications — and for most people it is also how you sign in. Your personal address is kept alongside it so you can still be reached if the LCM one is not working. Some officers sign in with a role address instead (secretary@, treasurer@, and so on); that is deliberate, because the role’s powers are attached to it.

Nothing is in my menu that this guide mentions.

Then that duty is not yours, which is normal — the menu is built from your role. If you believe you should have something and do not, ask Finance to check your record under Access & Roles rather than assuming a fault.
